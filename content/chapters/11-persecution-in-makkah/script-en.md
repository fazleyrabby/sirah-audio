# Persecution in Makkah

## s01 -- Those Without Protection
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

In Makkah, a person's safety came from his clan.
Those who had no clan had no safety. <!-- c11-001 -->

His Companion Abdullah ibn Mas'ud described what happened to them.
He said that the first to make their Islam known were seven: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Abu Bakr, Ammar and his mother Sumayyah, Suhayb, Bilal, and al-Miqdad. <!-- c11-001 -->

The Messenger of Allah was protected by his uncle, Abu Talib.
Abu Bakr was protected by his people.
As for the others, the idolaters seized them.
They dressed them in coats of iron, and left them out in the heat of the sun. <!-- c11-001 -->

## s02 -- Bilal
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Ibn Mas'ud said that every one of them, under that torment, gave their captors the words they wanted to hear.
Every one, except Bilal. <!-- c11-001 -->

Bilal was an Abyssinian, and a slave.
His own life meant little to him for the sake of Allah, Ibn Mas'ud said, and it meant little to his people.
They handed him over to the boys of the town, who dragged him through the streets of Makkah.
And he kept saying: "One. One." <!-- c11-001 -->

It was Abu Bakr who bought Bilal, and set him free.
Years later, Umar would say: "Abu Bakr is our master, and he freed our master." <!-- c11-002 -->

## s03 -- The Family of Yasir
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-left | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Ammar, his father Yasir, and his mother Sumayyah were among those who were tortured. <!-- c11-003 -->

The historians of the Seerah report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ passed by them, and could do nothing for them but speak.
It is reported that he said: "Patience, family of Yasir. Your appointed place is Paradise." <!-- c11-003 -->

They report that Sumayyah was killed, and that she was the first to be killed for this faith. <!-- c11-004 -->

Ammar himself, it is reported, was tormented until he said with his tongue what his heart rejected.
He came to the Messenger of Allah in distress. <!-- c11-005 -->
The Qur'an speaks of such a person.
Its meaning is: "Except for one who is forced, while his heart is at rest in faith." <!-- c11-006 -->

## s04 -- You Are in Haste
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-right | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Khabbab was a blacksmith who suffered persecution. <!-- c11-012 -->

He said: "I came to the Prophet while he was resting on his cloak in the shade of the Ka'bah.
We had suffered greatly from the idolaters.
I said: Will you not call upon Allah for us?" <!-- c11-007 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ sat up, and his face was flushed.
He said: "Among those who came before you, a man would be raked with combs of iron, down to the bone, and it would not turn him from his religion.
A saw would be set upon the parting of his head, and he would be split in two, and it would not turn him from his religion.
Allah will complete this matter, until a rider travels from Sana'a to Hadramawt fearing none but Allah." <!-- c11-007 -->

And in another narration of the same words, he added: "But you are in haste." <!-- c11-007 -->

## s05 -- At the Ka'bah
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

His uncle's protection kept the Messenger of Allah ﷺ alive.
It did not keep him from harm. <!-- c11-001 c11-008 -->

Ibn Mas'ud said that he was once praying beside the Ka'bah, while Abu Jahl and his companions sat nearby.
One of them asked who would bring the entrails of a slaughtered camel, and put them on his back when he prostrated.
The most wretched of them went and brought them.
He waited until the Prophet was in prostration, and laid them between his shoulders. <!-- c11-008 -->

They laughed until they fell against one another.
Ibn Mas'ud said: "I was watching, and I could do nothing. If only I had had someone to protect me."
The Prophet stayed in prostration, and did not raise his head, until his daughter Fatimah came, and took it off his back. <!-- c11-008 -->

On another day, a man named Uqbah ibn Abi Mu'ayt came up to him while he was praying, wound his garment around his neck, and strangled him with it.
Abu Bakr came, seized Uqbah by the shoulder, and pushed him away.
He said: "Would you kill a man because he says: My Lord is Allah?" <!-- c11-009 -->

## s06 -- A Trench of Fire
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Abu Jahl swore that if he saw him put his face to the ground in prayer, he would tread on his neck. <!-- c11-010 -->

He came toward him while he was praying.
Then the people saw him turn back on his heels, warding something off with his hands.
They asked him what was wrong.
He said: "Between me and him there is a trench of fire, and terror, and wings." <!-- c11-010 -->

The Qur'an had told the believers what to expect.
Its meaning is:
"Do people think that they will be left to say: We believe, and that they will not be tested?" <!-- c11-011 -->
