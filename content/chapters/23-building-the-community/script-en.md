# Building the Community

## s01 -- The Mosque
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The first thing he built in Madinah was a mosque. <!-- c23-001 -->

His Companion Anas described the ground where the camel had knelt.
There were old graves of idolaters in it, and uneven patches, and some date palms.
The graves were dug up, the ground was levelled, and the palms were cut down.
Their trunks were set in rows, to mark the direction of prayer. <!-- c23-001 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ worked alongside them, carrying bricks. <!-- c23-002 -->
And as they worked, they chanted together, and he chanted with them:
"O Allah, there is no good but the good of the Hereafter.
So help the Ansar, and the Emigrants." <!-- c23-001 -->

It was a simple building: walls of unbaked brick, pillars of palm trunk, a roof of palm branches. <!-- c23-003 -->
Along one side, rooms were built for his family. <!-- c23-003 -->

## s02 -- The Call to Prayer
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a town among mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

At first, the believers had no way of calling one another to prayer.
His Companion Ibn Umar said that they would gather, and guess at the time. <!-- c23-004 -->

They talked it over.
Some suggested a bell, like the Christians.
Others suggested a horn, like the Jews. <!-- c23-004 -->

Then one of the Ansar, Abdullah ibn Zayd, came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and told him of a dream.
A man had appeared to him in his sleep, and taught him words with which to call the people. <!-- c23-005 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "It is a true vision, if Allah wills.
Go with Bilal, and teach him what you saw, and let him call with it.
For his voice carries further than yours." <!-- c23-005 -->

So the first voice to call the believers to prayer was the voice of Bilal:
the man who had been dragged through the streets of Makkah, saying "One. One." <!-- c23-006 -->

## s03 -- Brothers
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The Emigrants had arrived with nothing.
The people of Madinah, whom he named the Ansar, the Helpers, had land and houses. <!-- c23-007 -->

He joined them together, in pairs: one of the Emigrants, and one of the Ansar, as brothers. <!-- c23-008 -->

Abd ar-Rahman ibn Awf told what happened to him.
He was paired with Sa'd ibn ar-Rabi, one of the wealthiest of the Ansar.
Sa'd said to him: "I will divide my wealth with you, half and half." <!-- c23-008 -->

Abd ar-Rahman answered: "May Allah bless you in your family and your wealth.
Show me the way to the market." <!-- c23-008 -->

He went, and traded, and came back that day with a little dried curd and butter that he had earned.
And before long, he needed nothing from anyone. <!-- c23-008 -->

## s04 -- Those Who Preferred Others
Image: desert/dunes-night.svg | slow-zoom | Desert dunes at night under a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Anas said that the Ansar shared their date palms with the Emigrants, who worked the land with them and took half the fruit. <!-- c23-007 -->

The Qur'an describes the Ansar.
Its meaning is:
"They love those who emigrated to them.
They find no want in their hearts for what the others have been given.
And they prefer them over themselves, even when they themselves are in need." <!-- c23-009 -->

There were also men in Madinah who had no family there, and no house.
They lived in a shaded part of the mosque, and were known as the people of the Bench.
Abu Hurayrah, who was one of them, called them the guests of Islam. <!-- c23-010 -->

## s05 -- The Direction of Prayer
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

For sixteen or seventeen months after he came to Madinah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ prayed facing Jerusalem.
But he loved to face the Ka'bah. <!-- c23-011 -->

Then a verse was revealed.
Its meaning is:
"We have seen the turning of your face toward the heaven.
So We shall turn you toward a direction that will please you.
Turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque.
And wherever you are, turn your faces toward it." <!-- c23-012 -->

Al-Bara said that a man who had prayed with him then passed by some of the Ansar, who were praying toward Jerusalem.
He told them that he had just prayed with the Prophet, facing the Ka'bah.
And they turned, as they were, in the middle of their prayer. <!-- c23-011 -->

The mosque, the call to prayer, the bond of brotherhood, and the direction of prayer were now part of the community's life. <!-- c23-001 c23-004 c23-008 c23-011 -->
