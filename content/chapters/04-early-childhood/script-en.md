# His Early Childhood

## s01 -- Among Banu Sa'd
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The first years of his life were spent in the desert, in the care of his nurse Halimah, among the tribe of Banu Sa'd. <!-- c04-001 -->

The historians report that Halimah spoke of a blessing that came to her household while the child was with her,
and that when the time came to return him to his mother, she asked to keep him longer. <!-- c04-002 -->

## s02 -- The Opening of the Chest
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

It was there, among Banu Sa'd, that something happened which the Companion Anas ibn Malik later narrated. <!-- c04-003 -->

The child was playing with other boys when the angel Jibril came to him.
He took hold of him and laid him on the ground.
He opened his chest and took out his heart, and removed a clot from it, and said: "This was the portion of Satan in you."
Then he washed the heart in a basin of gold, with the water of Zamzam, and returned it to its place. <!-- c04-003 -->

The other boys ran to his nurse, crying that Muhammad had been killed.
They hurried to him, and found him with his colour changed.
Anas said: "I used to see the mark of that stitching on his chest." <!-- c04-003 -->

After this, the historians report, Halimah was afraid for him, and took him back to his mother in Makkah. <!-- c04-004 -->

## s03 -- Aminah
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He lived with his mother, Aminah, until he was about six years old. <!-- c04-005 -->

Then, as the Seerah reports, she took him north to Yathrib, to visit his relatives of the clan of Najjar.
With them was her servant, Umm Ayman.
On the journey home, Aminah fell ill.
She died at a place called al-Abwa, on the road between Yathrib and Makkah.
It was Umm Ayman who brought the boy back. <!-- c04-006 -->

Many years later, he visited his mother's grave.
He wept, and those who were with him wept. <!-- c04-007 -->

## s04 -- Abd al-Muttalib
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

His grandfather, Abd al-Muttalib, took him in.
The historians report that the old leader of Quraysh showed him an affection he showed no one else,
and would seat the boy beside him on his own mat, in the shade of the Ka'bah. <!-- c04-008 -->

But Abd al-Muttalib was very old.
He died when his grandson was eight. <!-- c04-009 -->

## s05 -- Abu Talib
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-left | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

His care now passed to his uncle Abu Talib, the full brother of his father.
Abu Talib was not a wealthy man, and he had many children of his own.
But he took the boy into his house, and he would stand by him for the rest of his life. <!-- c04-010 -->

His father had died before he was born.
His mother died when he was six.
His grandfather died when he was eight. <!-- c04-009 -->

The Qur'an would later speak to him of these years.
Its meaning is:
"Did He not find you an orphan, and give you shelter?
And He found you in need, and made you free of need." <!-- c04-011 -->
