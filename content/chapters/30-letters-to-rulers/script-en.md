# Letters to Rulers

## s01 -- Beyond Arabia
Image: maps/arabia.svg | static | A map of the Arabian peninsula between the Byzantine and Persian empires, with Abyssinia across the Red Sea.
Audio: Voice only.

The Qur'an says of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ:
"We have not sent you except to all people, as a bringer of good news and a warner." <!-- c30-001 -->

During the truce of Hudaybiyyah, a letter from him reached the Roman emperor Heraclius. <!-- c30-002 -->

Anas narrated that he wrote to Chosroes, the king of Persia, to Caesar, the emperor of the Romans, to the Negus, and to every ruler of power, calling them to Allah. <!-- c30-002 -->

## s02 -- The Seal
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

When he wished to write to the Romans, Anas said, he was told that they would not read a letter unless it bore a seal. <!-- c30-003 -->

So he had a ring made, of silver.
On it were engraved three words: Muhammad, Messenger of Allah. <!-- c30-003 -->

Anas said: "It is as though I can still see its whiteness on his hand." <!-- c30-003 -->

## s03 -- The Letter to Heraclius
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The letter to the Roman emperor, Heraclius, has come down to us word for word. <!-- c30-004 -->

"In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
From Muhammad, the servant of Allah and His Messenger, to Heraclius, the great one of the Romans.
Peace be upon whoever follows the guidance." <!-- c30-004 -->

"I call you with the call of Islam.
Accept Islam, and you will be safe, and Allah will give you your reward twice over.
But if you turn away, then upon you is the sin of your subjects." <!-- c30-004 -->

Then it quoted a verse of the Qur'an.
Its meaning is:
"O People of the Book, come to a word that is common between us and you:
that we worship none but Allah,
that we associate nothing with Him,
and that none of us takes others as lords beside Allah." <!-- c30-005 -->

## s04 -- The Emperor's Questions
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Heraclius was in Jerusalem when the letter reached him.
He ordered that any men of the Prophet's people who were in the land be brought before him.
A caravan of Quraysh was found, and at its head was Abu Sufyan, who was then still his enemy. <!-- c30-006 -->

Abu Sufyan later told the story himself.
Heraclius asked him about the lineage of this man, and he said that it was noble.
He asked whether any of his forefathers had been a king. No.
Whether he had ever been known to lie, before he said what he said. No.
Whether he broke his promises. No.
What he commanded them to do.
Abu Sufyan answered: "He tells us to worship Allah alone, and to leave what our fathers worshipped.
He commands us to pray, to speak the truth, to be chaste, and to keep the ties of kinship." <!-- c30-006 -->

And Abu Sufyan said: "By Allah, had I not been ashamed that my companions would report a lie from me, I would have lied about him." <!-- c30-006 -->

Heraclius said: "If what you say is true, he will come to rule the ground beneath these two feet of mine.
I knew that he was going to appear, but I did not think he would be from among you.
If I knew that I could reach him, I would go to great lengths to meet him.
And if I were with him, I would wash his feet." <!-- c30-007 -->

But when he tested his court, they turned on him in uproar.
And he did not follow what he had said. <!-- c30-007 -->

## s05 -- The King of Persia
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The letter to Chosroes, king of Persia, was carried by Abdullah ibn Hudhafah.
When Chosroes had read it, he tore it to pieces. <!-- c30-008 -->

When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ heard of it, the narrator says, he prayed that they would be torn apart, utterly. <!-- c30-008 -->

The historians of the Seerah report other letters as well.
One went to the ruler of Egypt, who did not accept Islam, but answered with courtesy, and sent gifts.
One went to the Negus of Abyssinia. <!-- c30-009 -->

Within a generation, the empire of Chosroes had ceased to exist. <!-- c30-010 -->

His letters carried the invitation to rulers beyond Arabia. <!-- c30-002 -->
