# Cave Thawr

## s01 -- Three Nights
Image: mountains/hira-night.svg | slow-zoom | A bare mountain under a night sky with a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Mount Thawr stands a few miles south of Makkah.
It is higher and steeper than Hira, and its cave is small, and hard to reach. <!-- c20-001 -->

For three nights, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and Abu Bakr stayed inside it,
while Quraysh searched every road out of the town. <!-- c20-002 -->

## s02 -- Those Who Came and Went
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-left | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

They were not alone in the plan.
Aishah described how her family kept them supplied, and informed. <!-- c20-003 -->

Her brother Abdullah, the son of Abu Bakr, was a quick and perceptive young man.
He would spend the night with them at the cave.
Before dawn he would slip away, so that in the morning he was among Quraysh, as though he had slept in Makkah.
Whatever he heard being plotted against them, he remembered.
And when darkness fell, he brought it to them. <!-- c20-003 -->

Amir ibn Fuhayrah, a freed slave of Abu Bakr, grazed a flock of sheep nearby.
Each evening, a while after nightfall, he would drive the flock to the cave, so that they had fresh milk.
And while it was still dark, he would call the flock away again.
He did this on each of the three nights. <!-- c20-003 -->

## s03 -- The Search
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Quraysh, meanwhile, sent word to the tribes around Makkah.
Whoever killed or captured either of the two men would receive the price of a man's blood. <!-- c20-004 -->

The searchers followed the tracks south.
They climbed Thawr.
And they came to the mouth of the cave. <!-- c20-005 -->

## s04 -- The Third of Them
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Abu Bakr himself narrated what happened then. <!-- c20-005 -->

He said: "I was with the Prophet in the cave.
I raised my head, and I could see the feet of the people.
I said: Messenger of Allah, if one of them were to look down at his feet, he would see us." <!-- c20-005 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"Abu Bakr, what do you think of two, when Allah is the third of them?" <!-- c20-005 -->

The Qur'an records that moment.
Its meaning is:
"Allah helped him when those who disbelieved drove him out, the second of two,
when they were both in the cave, and he said to his companion: Do not grieve. Allah is with us.
Then Allah sent down His calm upon him, and supported him with forces you did not see." <!-- c20-006 -->

The searchers did not look down.
They went away. <!-- c20-005 -->

## s05 -- The Spider and the Doves
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-left | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Many people know a story about that cave: that a spider spun its web across the entrance, and two doves nested there, so that the searchers thought it empty. <!-- c20-007 -->

The reports of this have been graded weak by a number of scholars of hadith, though some have accepted the report of the spider.
We mention it because it is so widely told, and we do not relate it as established. <!-- c20-007 -->

What the Qur'an itself says is that he was supported with forces that were not seen. <!-- c20-006 -->

## s06 -- The Guide Returns
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.



On the morning they had agreed, the guide came to the cave with the two camels.
He was a man who shared the religion of their enemies, and who knew of the reward.
He kept his word. <!-- c20-008 -->

They set out: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Abu Bakr, Amir ibn Fuhayrah, and the guide.
And he led them by the road along the coast. <!-- c20-008 -->
