# The Final Days

## s01 -- The Illness Begins
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He returned from the pilgrimage to Madinah.
About three months later, at the beginning of the eleventh year, he fell ill. <!-- c35-001 -->

It began with a pain in the head.
His wife Aishah said that she herself had a headache that day, and said: "Oh, my head."
And he said: "Rather, it is I who should say: Oh, my head." <!-- c35-002 -->

In those same days he had prepared an expedition toward the lands of the north, and placed at its head a young man: Usamah, the son of Zayd.
Some people objected to his being given command.
He rose and said: "If you find fault with his command, you found fault with the command of his father before him.
By Allah, he was worthy of command, and he was among the dearest of people to me.
And this one is among the dearest of people to me after him." <!-- c35-003 -->

## s02 -- The House of Aishah
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-right | A town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

When the illness grew heavy, Aishah said, he asked permission of his wives to be nursed in her house.
They gave it. <!-- c35-004 -->

He came out supported between two men, his feet trailing on the ground. <!-- c35-004 -->

He said to Aishah, in that illness:
"I still feel the pain of the food I ate at Khaybar." <!-- c35-005 -->

## s03 -- A Servant Was Given a Choice
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

One day he came out to the people, and sat on the pulpit. <!-- c35-006 -->

He said: "Allah gave one of His servants a choice between this world, and what is with Him.
And the servant chose what is with Allah." <!-- c35-006 -->

Abu Bakr wept.
His Companion Abu Sa'id said: "We wondered at his weeping, that the Messenger of Allah should tell of a servant who was given a choice.
But the Messenger of Allah was himself the one given the choice.
And Abu Bakr was the most knowing of us." <!-- c35-006 -->

Then he said: "The one who has been most generous to me, in his companionship and his wealth, is Abu Bakr.
If I were to take any close friend other than my Lord, I would take Abu Bakr.
But there is the brotherhood of Islam, and its love.
Let no door into the mosque remain open, except the door of Abu Bakr." <!-- c35-006 -->

## s04 -- Tell Abu Bakr to Lead the Prayer
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a town among mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

When he became too weak to go out for the prayer, and the call was made, he said:
"Tell Abu Bakr to lead the people in prayer." <!-- c35-007 -->

Aishah said that Abu Bakr was a tender-hearted man, and that if he stood in that place he would not be able to make the people hear, for weeping.
He repeated his instruction.
And Abu Bakr led the prayer through the days of his illness. <!-- c35-007 -->

He gave his people counsel in those days.
He spoke of the Ansar: "Accept the good of those among them who do good, and overlook those among them who do wrong." <!-- c35-008 -->

And Aishah said that he warned against what earlier peoples had done, who took the graves of their prophets as places of worship.
"Were it not for that," she said, "his grave would have been made prominent." <!-- c35-009 -->

## s05 -- Fatimah
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

His daughter Fatimah came to him. <!-- c35-010 -->

Aishah said that she walked exactly as he walked.
He welcomed her, and seated her beside him.
Then he whispered something to her, and she wept.
He whispered to her again, and she laughed. <!-- c35-010 -->

Aishah asked her afterwards what he had said.
Fatimah would not tell her, while he lived. <!-- c35-010 -->

After his death, she told.
The first time, he had told her that his time had come.
The second time, he had told her that she would be the first of his family to follow him.
"And he said: Are you not content to be the foremost of the women of Paradise?
So I laughed." <!-- c35-010 -->

## s06 -- Monday Morning
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty land at dawn, light spreading. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Anas told of the last morning. <!-- c35-011 -->

"Abu Bakr was leading them in prayer, in the illness of which the Prophet died.
When it was Monday, and they were standing in their rows, the Prophet drew back the curtain of the room.
He stood looking at us.
His face was like a page of the Qur'an.
Then he smiled." <!-- c35-011 -->

"We were almost distracted from our prayer, for joy at seeing him.
Abu Bakr stepped back to join the row, thinking he would come out.
But he gestured to us: Complete your prayer.
And he let the curtain fall." <!-- c35-011 -->

He had seen his community standing together in prayer, without him.
It was the last time they saw him. <!-- c35-011 -->
