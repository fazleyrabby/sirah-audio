# The Lineage of Muhammad ﷺ

## s01 -- A Name and Its Fathers
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-left | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Among the Arabs, a person was known by his fathers.
Lineage was memory, and it was carefully kept. <!-- c02-001 -->

The lineage of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ has been preserved, father by father, for twenty-one generations.
He is Muhammad, son of Abdullah, son of Abd al-Muttalib, son of Hashim, son of Abd Manaf, son of Qusayy.
The line continues through Kilab, Murrah, Ka'b, Lu'ayy, Ghalib, Fihr, Malik, and an-Nadr,
through Kinanah, Khuzaymah, Mudrikah, Ilyas, Mudar, Nizar, and Ma'add,
up to Adnan.
On this much, the scholars of genealogy agree. <!-- c02-002 -->

Beyond Adnan, they agree that the line goes back to Ismail, the son of Ibrahim, peace be upon them both.
But the names in between are disputed, and we do not recite them here. <!-- c02-003 -->

## s02 -- The Valley of Ismail
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over an empty valley ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

How the family of Ismail came to Makkah was told by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself, in a long narration recorded by al-Bukhari. <!-- c02-004 -->

Ibrahim brought Hajar and her infant son Ismail to the place of the Ka'bah.
At that time there was no one in Makkah, and no water.
He left them there with a bag of dates and a skin of water, and turned to go.
She followed him, asking where he was going, leaving them in that empty valley.
He did not turn back.
Then she asked: "Has Allah commanded you to do this?"
He said: "Yes."
She said: "Then He will not neglect us." <!-- c02-004 -->

When the water was gone, she climbed the hill of Safa to look for help, and then the hill of Marwah.
She went between them seven times.
Then, at the place of Zamzam, she found the angel, and water flowed from the ground. <!-- c02-004 -->

Some time later, people of the tribe of Jurhum passed by.
They saw a bird circling, and knew there must be water.
They asked her permission to settle there, and she gave it.
Ismail grew up among them, learned Arabic from them, and married among them. <!-- c02-004 -->

And when Ibrahim returned, father and son raised the foundations of the House together. <!-- c02-005 -->

The Messenger of Allah once passed some men practising archery, and called to them:
"Shoot, children of Ismail, for your father was an archer." <!-- c02-006 -->

## s03 -- Quraysh
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-left | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Generations passed.
Among the descendants of Adnan was Kinanah, and from Kinanah came Fihr.
The historians report that it is the descendants of Fihr who are called Quraysh. <!-- c02-007 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"Allah chose Kinanah from the children of Ismail.
He chose Quraysh from Kinanah.
He chose the clan of Hashim from Quraysh.
And He chose me from the clan of Hashim." <!-- c02-008 -->

Five generations before him lived Qusayy.
The Seerah reports that Quraysh had been scattered, and that it was Qusayy who gathered them into Makkah and took the custodianship of the Ka'bah.
From his time, the keys of the House, the giving of water and food to the pilgrims, and the council of Makkah were in the hands of Quraysh. <!-- c02-009 -->

## s04 -- Hashim
Image: maps/arabia.svg | static | A map of the Arabian peninsula with Makkah, Yathrib and the caravan route between Yemen and Syria.
Audio: Voice only.

Qusayy's grandson was named Amr. He is remembered as Hashim.
The historians say the name came from a year of hunger, when he broke bread into broth to feed the people of Makkah.
Hashim means the one who breaks. <!-- c02-010 -->

They also report that it was Hashim who established the two great caravan journeys of Quraysh:
to Yemen in the winter, and to Syria in the summer. <!-- c02-011 -->

These are the journeys the Qur'an mentions, when it calls on Quraysh to worship the Lord of this House,
who fed them against hunger, and made them safe from fear. <!-- c02-012 -->

On one journey north, the historians report, Hashim stopped in Yathrib and married Salma, a woman of the clan of Najjar.
He travelled on, and died in Gaza.
Salma gave birth to his son in Yathrib.
The boy was named Shaybah. <!-- c02-013 -->

## s05 -- Abd al-Muttalib
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-right | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Shaybah grew up in Yathrib, among his mother's people.
The Seerah reports that his uncle, al-Muttalib, came and brought him back to Makkah, riding behind him on his camel.
Those who saw them took the boy for a servant al-Muttalib had acquired, and called him Abd al-Muttalib: the servant of al-Muttalib.
The name remained with him. <!-- c02-014 -->

Abd al-Muttalib became the leader of Quraysh. <!-- c02-015 -->
The historians report that the well of Zamzam had been buried and forgotten for generations,
and that it was he who found its place, and dug it open again. <!-- c02-016 -->

Many years later, on a day of battle, his grandson would say:
"I am the Prophet, it is no lie. I am the son of Abd al-Muttalib." <!-- c02-017 -->

## s06 -- A Noble Family
Image: desert/dunes-night.svg | slow-zoom | Desert dunes at night under a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

This lineage was acknowledged even by those who opposed him.
Abu Sufyan, at a time when he was still an enemy of the Prophet, was questioned about him by the Roman emperor Heraclius.
The first question was about his lineage.
Abu Sufyan answered: "He is of noble lineage among us."
Heraclius later remarked that this is how messengers are sent: from the noble families of their people. <!-- c02-018 -->

Among the sons of Abd al-Muttalib was Abdullah.
The Seerah reports that Abd al-Muttalib chose for him a bride from the clan of Zuhrah: Aminah, the daughter of Wahb.
Their marriage would be brief. <!-- c02-019 -->

And their son would be the Messenger of Allah, and the seal of the prophets. <!-- c02-020 -->
