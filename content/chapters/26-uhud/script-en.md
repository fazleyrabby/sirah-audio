# Uhud

## s01 -- Quraysh Return
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-right | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Quraysh did not accept what had happened at Badr. <!-- c26-001 -->

A year later, in the third year after the Hijrah, the historians of the Seerah report that they marched on Madinah with three thousand men. <!-- c26-001 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ went out to meet them with about a thousand.
On the way, the historians report, Abdullah ibn Ubayy turned back, and took a third of the army with him. <!-- c26-002 -->

The Qur'an speaks of those who turned back that day.
Its meaning is:
"They were told: Come, fight in the way of Allah, or defend.
They said: If we knew there would be fighting, we would follow you.
They were nearer that day to disbelief than to faith." <!-- c26-003 -->

The Muslims who remained took their position at the foot of Mount Uhud, north of Madinah. <!-- c26-001 -->

## s02 -- The Archers
Image: mountains/hira-night.svg | pan-left | A bare mountain against the sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Behind the Muslim lines there was a small hill.
The Messenger of Allah ﷺ placed fifty archers on it, under the command of Abdullah ibn Jubayr. <!-- c26-004 -->

Al-Bara narrated the order he gave them.
"Hold your position.
Do not leave it, even if you see the birds snatching at us, until I send for you.
And if you see that we have defeated them, do not leave it, until I send for you." <!-- c26-004 -->

## s03 -- The Hill Is Left
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Open ground under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

At first, the battle went the way of the Muslims.
Al-Bara said: "They fled, until I saw their women running up the slope." <!-- c26-005 -->

The archers on the hill saw it too.
They began to say: "The spoils! The spoils!"
Abdullah ibn Jubayr said: "Have you forgotten what the Messenger of Allah told you?"
But most of them went down. <!-- c26-005 -->

The historians report that the cavalry of Quraysh saw the hill left open, rode around it, and came upon the Muslims from behind. <!-- c26-006 -->

The Qur'an speaks of that moment.
Its meaning is:
"Allah had fulfilled His promise to you, when you were overcoming them by His leave,
until you lost heart, and disputed about the order, and disobeyed, after He had shown you what you love.
Among you are some who desire this world, and among you are some who desire the Hereafter." <!-- c26-007 -->

## s04 -- The Messenger Wounded
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty ground at first light. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

In the confusion that followed, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ himself was struck. <!-- c26-008 -->

His Companion Sahl ibn Sa'd said that his face was wounded, one of his teeth was broken, and the helmet was crushed upon his head. <!-- c26-008 -->

A cry went up that he had been killed.
Some lost heart. <!-- c26-009 -->
The Qur'an addressed them.
Its meaning is:
"Muhammad is no more than a messenger. Messengers have passed away before him.
If he dies, or is killed, will you turn back on your heels?" <!-- c26-010 -->

Others gathered around him.
A man who later saw the hand of Talhah said that it was paralysed: he had shielded the Prophet with it that day. <!-- c26-011 -->

And Anas told of his uncle, Anas ibn an-Nadr, who had missed Badr, and had promised that Allah would see what he would do.
When the people fell back, he went forward.
He passed Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, and said: "Paradise, by the Lord of an-Nadr. I find its fragrance coming from Uhud."
They found him afterwards with more than eighty wounds.
Only his sister could recognise him, by his fingertips. <!-- c26-012 -->

## s05 -- After the Battle
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | slow-zoom | A valley at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Sahl said that it was Fatimah, the daughter of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, who washed the blood from his face, while Ali poured water from a shield.
When she saw that the water only made the bleeding worse, she burned a piece of matting, and pressed the ash into the wound, until it stopped. <!-- c26-008 -->

Seventy of the Muslims had been killed. <!-- c26-013 -->
Among them was Hamzah, the uncle of the Messenger of Allah. <!-- c26-014 -->

Before Quraysh withdrew, Abu Sufyan climbed a rise and called out.
"A day for the day of Badr. War goes by turns."
Then he cried: "Exalted be Hubal!"
The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Answer him."
They asked: "What shall we say?"
He said: "Say: Allah is higher, and more majestic." <!-- c26-015 -->

Abu Sufyan cried: "We have al-Uzza, and you have no Uzza."
He said: "Say: Allah is our protector, and you have no protector." <!-- c26-015 -->

## s06 -- What Uhud Taught
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Uhud was not a victory.
The Qur'an did not call it one.
It spoke to the believers about what had happened, and why. <!-- c26-016 -->

Its meaning is:
"Do not lose heart, and do not grieve. You will have the upper hand, if you are believers.
If a wound has touched you, a like wound has touched the others.
Such days We deal out in turn among the people." <!-- c26-016 -->

And of those who had been killed, it said:
"Do not think of those who were killed in the way of Allah as dead.
They are alive, with their Lord, and provided for." <!-- c26-017 -->

Years later, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ looked toward that mountain, and said:
"Uhud is a mountain that loves us, and we love it." <!-- c26-018 -->
