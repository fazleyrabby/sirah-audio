# The Birth of Muhammad ﷺ

## s01 -- The Year of the Elephant
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-right | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The historians of the Seerah report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was born in the year the Arabs came to call the Year of the Elephant. <!-- c03-001 -->

It took its name from an event the Qur'an describes in a short chapter.
Its meaning is: <!-- c03-002 -->

"Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with the people of the elephant?
Did He not make their plot go astray?
He sent against them birds in flocks,
striking them with stones of baked clay,
and He made them like eaten straw." <!-- c03-002 -->

The historians give the background.
They report that Abraha, the Abyssinian ruler of Yemen, marched on Makkah with an army that included an elephant, intending to destroy the Ka'bah. <!-- c03-003 -->

They also report that Abraha's men seized camels belonging to Abd al-Muttalib, the leader of Quraysh,
and that he went to ask for them back.
When Abraha expressed surprise that he spoke of his camels and not of the House,
he is reported to have replied that he was the owner of the camels, and that the House had a Lord who would protect it. <!-- c03-004 -->

The army never reached the Ka'bah.
What became of it is told in the verses we have just heard. <!-- c03-002 -->

## s02 -- The Family
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Abd al-Muttalib was the grandfather of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.
The family belonged to the clan of Hashim, within the tribe of Quraysh. <!-- c03-005 -->

The Messenger of Allah later said that Allah chose Kinanah from the descendants of Ismail,
chose Quraysh from Kinanah,
chose the clan of Hashim from Quraysh,
and chose him from the clan of Hashim. <!-- c03-006 -->

The Seerah records that his father was Abdullah, the son of Abd al-Muttalib,
and that his mother was Aminah, the daughter of Wahb, of the clan of Zuhrah. <!-- c03-007 -->

According to the most widely held report, Abdullah died on a journey, in Yathrib, before his son was born. <!-- c03-008 -->

The child would come into the world an orphan.
The Qur'an would later remind him of this.
Its meaning is: "Did He not find you an orphan, and give you shelter?" <!-- c03-009 -->

## s03 -- A Monday in Makkah
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He was born in Makkah, on a Monday.
We know the day from his own words.
When he was asked about fasting on Mondays, he said:
"That is the day on which I was born, and the day on which revelation was sent down to me." <!-- c03-010 -->

The month, the historians report, was Rabi' al-Awwal. <!-- c03-011 -->
The exact date within that month is not agreed upon.
The twelfth is the most widely known.
Other scholars, among them the author of The Sealed Nectar, give the ninth.
In the common calendar, this falls around the year five hundred and seventy, or five hundred and seventy-one. <!-- c03-012 -->

Many stories are told about wonders on the night of his birth.
We do not relate them here, because their chains of narration are not established. <!-- c03-013 -->

The Seerah reports that it was his grandfather, Abd al-Muttalib, who named him Muhammad,
a name that was rare among the Arabs at that time. <!-- c03-014 -->

## s04 -- The Desert of Banu Sa'd
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He was nursed first, for a short time, by a woman named Thuwaybah. <!-- c03-015 -->

Then, as the historians report, he was entrusted to a nurse from the desert: Halimah, of the tribe of Banu Sa'd. <!-- c03-016 -->

This was a custom among the families of the towns.
Infants were sent to the Bedouin, to grow strong in the open air of the desert,
and to learn the pure Arabic that was spoken there. <!-- c03-017 -->

And so the first years of his life were spent not in Makkah, but among the tents of Banu Sa'd. <!-- c03-016 -->

What those early years held is the subject of the chapters that follow. <!-- n -->
