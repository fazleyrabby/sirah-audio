# Khaybar

## s01 -- The Forts of the North
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Some days' journey north of Madinah lay the oasis of Khaybar: rich in date palms, and guarded by a chain of stone forts. <!-- c29-001 -->

The historians of the Seerah report that it was from Khaybar that the Confederates had been stirred up and brought against Madinah, two years before. <!-- c29-002 -->

With the peace of Hudaybiyyah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was free of the threat from Quraysh in the south.
At the beginning of the seventh year after the Hijrah, the historians report, he marched north. <!-- c29-002 -->

## s02 -- The Morning
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Bare ground in the first light of morning. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

They travelled by night.
His Companion Salamah said that as they rode, a man of the company recited verses to keep the camels in step:
"O Allah, had it not been for You, we would not have been guided.
We would not have given charity, and we would not have prayed." <!-- c29-003 -->

Anas said: "We prayed the dawn prayer near Khaybar, while it was still dark."
Then they rode in.
The people of Khaybar were coming out to their fields, with their spades and their baskets.
When they saw him, they cried: "Muhammad, and the army!" <!-- c29-004 -->

## s03 -- The Banner
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-right | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The forts did not fall quickly. <!-- c29-005 -->

One evening, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"Tomorrow I will give this banner to a man at whose hands Allah will grant victory.
He loves Allah and His Messenger, and Allah and His Messenger love him." <!-- c29-006 -->

His Companion Sahl said that the people spent that night wondering which of them it would be.
In the morning they came to him, each one hoping.
He said: "Where is Ali, the son of Abu Talib?"
They said: "Messenger of Allah, his eyes are troubling him."
He sent for him, and prayed for him, and his eyes were healed, as though there had never been any pain in them.
And he gave him the banner. <!-- c29-006 -->

Ali asked: "Messenger of Allah, shall I fight them until they are as we are?"
He said: "Go forward, without haste, until you come to their ground.
Then call them to Islam, and tell them what is due from them to Allah.
By Allah, that Allah should guide one man through you is better for you than the finest camels." <!-- c29-006 -->

## s04 -- The Terms
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-left | Open ground under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

When the forts had been taken, the people of Khaybar asked for terms. <!-- c29-007 -->

They knew the land, and how to work it.
His Companion Ibn Umar narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ left them on their land, to cultivate it, in return for half of what it yielded. <!-- c29-007 -->

Among the captives of Khaybar was Safiyyah, the daughter of one of its leaders.
Anas narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ set her free, and married her. <!-- c29-008 -->

It was at Khaybar, too, that a woman of the town brought him a roasted sheep that she had poisoned.
He ate a little of it.
She was brought before him, and his Companions asked: "Shall we not kill her?"
He said: "No." <!-- c29-009 -->

## s05 -- The People of the Ship
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

While he was still at Khaybar, travellers arrived whom he had not seen for many years. <!-- c29-010 -->

Ja'far, the son of Abu Talib, had come back from Abyssinia, with the last of those who had taken refuge there.
With them were men from Yemen who had set out to join the Prophet years before, and whose ship had carried them to Abyssinia instead. <!-- c29-010 -->

Some in Madinah said to these latecomers: "We emigrated before you."
One of the women who had come from Abyssinia, Asma bint Umays, went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and told him what was being said. <!-- c29-010 -->

He said: "They have no greater claim to me than you.
They have one emigration.
And you, the people of the ship, have two." <!-- c29-010 -->

Ja'far and his party had stayed in Abyssinia; now they reached him at Khaybar. <!-- c29-010 -->
