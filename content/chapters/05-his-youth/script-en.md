# His Youth

## s01 -- The Shepherd
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He grew up in the house of his uncle Abu Talib, and as a boy he worked.
We know what that work was from his own words. <!-- c05-001 -->

He once said: "Allah did not send any prophet but that he tended sheep."
His Companions asked: "And you?"
He said: "Yes. I used to tend them for the people of Makkah, for a few small coins." <!-- c05-001 -->

## s02 -- The Road to Syria
Image: maps/arabia.svg | static | A map of the Arabian peninsula with Makkah, Yathrib and the caravan route north toward Syria.
Audio: Voice only.

The historians report that when he was about twelve, he travelled with Abu Talib in a trade caravan heading north, toward Syria. <!-- c05-002 -->

There is a well-known story about that journey: that a monk named Bahira recognised the boy as a future prophet.
Several scholars of hadith judged its chain of narration to be unsound.
So we mention that the story exists, and we do not narrate it. <!-- c05-003 -->

## s03 -- A War and a Pact
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-left | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

In his youth, war came to Makkah.
The historians report that Quraysh and their allies of Kinanah fought the tribes of Qays Aylan.
It was called the Sacrilegious War, because it was fought in the sacred months, when the Arabs held fighting to be forbidden.
They report that he was present with his uncles, and that he gathered arrows for them. <!-- c05-004 -->

When it was over, something better followed.
The Seerah reports that several clans of Quraysh met in the house of a man named Abdullah ibn Jud'an.
They pledged that they would stand with anyone who was wronged in Makkah, whether he was one of its people or a stranger, until his right was returned to him.
It became known as the Pact of the Virtuous. <!-- c05-005 -->

He was there.
And it is reported that many years later, as a prophet, he said he would not exchange that pact for the finest red camels,
and that if he were called to it in Islam, he would answer. <!-- c05-006 -->

## s04 -- The Trustworthy
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-right | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He lived among a people who worshipped idols.
The historians report that he took no part in it. <!-- c05-007 -->

And his people came to know him for one quality above all: he did not lie.
The proof of this comes from their own mouths. <!-- c05-008 -->

Years later, when he first called Quraysh together at the hill of Safa, he asked them:
"If I told you that horsemen were in the valley, about to attack you, would you believe me?"
They said: "Yes. We have never found you saying anything but the truth." <!-- c05-008 -->

And when the Roman emperor Heraclius asked Abu Sufyan, then his enemy, whether they had ever accused him of lying before he made his claim,
Abu Sufyan answered: "No." <!-- c05-009 -->

The historians report that in Makkah he was called al-Amin: the Trustworthy. <!-- c05-010 -->

## s05 -- A Young Man of Quraysh
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He was now a young man, of the noblest family in Makkah, and without wealth of his own.
And in Makkah there was a merchant, a woman named Khadijah, who sent men to trade with her goods,
and who was looking for someone she could trust. <!-- c05-011 -->
