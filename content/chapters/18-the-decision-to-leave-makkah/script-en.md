# The Decision to Leave Makkah

## s01 -- A Land of Date Palms
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

After the pledge at Aqabah, the believers in Makkah had a place to go. <!-- c18-001 -->

His wife Aishah narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to them:
"I have been shown the place of your emigration.
A land of date palms, between two tracts of black stone." <!-- c18-001 -->

He said in another narration that when he first saw it in a dream, he thought it might be al-Yamamah, or Hajar.
But it was Yathrib. <!-- c18-002 -->

## s02 -- They Leave
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | pan-right | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

So they began to leave, a few at a time. <!-- c18-001 -->

Al-Bara, who was a boy in Yathrib then, remembered the order in which they came.
"The first to come to us were Mus'ab ibn Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum.
Then came Bilal, and Sa'd, and Ammar ibn Yasir.
Then Umar ibn al-Khattab came, with twenty of the Companions of the Prophet." <!-- c18-003 -->

Many of those who had gone to Abyssinia years before now made their way to Yathrib as well. <!-- c18-001 -->

They went quietly, the historians report, leaving behind their houses, their trade, and whatever they could not carry.
Quraysh took what was left. <!-- c18-004 -->

## s03 -- What It Cost
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The historians preserve some of what the journey cost them. <!-- c18-005 -->

They report that when Abu Salamah set out with his wife and their small son, her clan stopped them, and would not let her go.
Then his clan came and took the child.
Abu Salamah went on to Yathrib alone.
His wife, Umm Salamah, remained in Makkah for about a year, separated from both, until the two clans relented. <!-- c18-005 -->

They report that Suhayb, who had come to Makkah poor and had grown wealthy there, was stopped on the road.
He told his pursuers where his wealth was hidden, and they let him pass.
And it is reported that when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ heard of it, he said: "Suhayb has profited." <!-- c18-006 -->

## s04 -- Wait
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-right | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Abu Bakr prepared to go as well. <!-- c18-007 -->

But the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to him: "Wait a while. I hope that I too will be given permission."
Abu Bakr said: "Do you hope for that? May my father be your ransom."
He said: "Yes." <!-- c18-007 -->

So Abu Bakr held himself back, to be his companion.
He had two riding camels, and for four months he fed them on the leaves of the acacia, to keep them ready. <!-- c18-007 -->

Before long, there were few believers left in Makkah besides the Messenger of Allah, Abu Bakr, Ali, and those who were held back by force. <!-- c18-008 -->

## s05 -- The Plot
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

His followers had begun leaving for Yathrib. Quraysh met in Dar an-Nadwah to decide what to do about him. <!-- c18-004 c18-010 -->

The Qur'an speaks of what they planned.
Its meaning is:
"When those who disbelieved plotted against you: to confine you, or to kill you, or to drive you out.
They plan, and Allah plans.
And Allah is the best of planners." <!-- c18-009 -->

The historians give the details.
They report that the leaders of Quraysh met in their council house, and weighed those three courses.
And they report that they settled on the second.
A young man would be chosen from each clan.
They would strike him together, so that the guilt of his blood would be spread among all the clans, and the sons of Hashim could not take revenge on all of them. <!-- c18-010 -->

That was their plan.
The permission he had been waiting for came first. <!-- c18-010 -->
