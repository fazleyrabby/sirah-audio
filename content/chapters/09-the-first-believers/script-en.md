# The First Believers

## s01 -- The First
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The first person to believe in him was the one who had covered him when he came down from the mountain.
The historians of the Seerah agree that it was his wife, Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her. <!-- c09-001 -->

After her, they name three from his own household and his closest circle.
Ali, the son of Abu Talib, who was a boy growing up in his home.
Zayd, the son of Harithah, whom he had set free and raised.
And his friend, Abu Bakr. <!-- c09-002 -->

The historians differ over which of the three came first.
Many of them put it this way: the first of the men was Abu Bakr, the first of the boys was Ali, and the first of the freed slaves was Zayd. <!-- c09-002 -->

## s02 -- Abu Bakr
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Years later, when some of his Companions had a disagreement with Abu Bakr, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ reminded them of those first days.
He said: "Allah sent me to you, and you said: You are lying.
And Abu Bakr said: He has spoken the truth.
And he supported me with himself and with his wealth.
So will you not leave my companion alone, for my sake?" <!-- c09-003 -->

Abu Bakr was a merchant, well known and well liked among Quraysh.
The Seerah reports that he began at once to speak to those he trusted,
and that through him came Uthman ibn Affan, az-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, Abd ar-Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, and Talhah ibn Ubaydullah. <!-- c09-004 -->

Sa'd later said of the day he accepted Islam:
"For seven days, I was a third of Islam." <!-- c09-005 -->

## s03 -- How Few
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

They were very few. <!-- c09-006 -->

Ammar, the son of Yasir, said:
"I saw the Messenger of Allah when there was no one with him but five slaves, two women, and Abu Bakr." <!-- c09-006 -->

When Amr ibn Abasah came to Makkah and asked him who was with him in this, he answered:
"A free man, and a slave." <!-- c09-007 -->
Amr said: "I will follow you."
He told him: "You cannot do that today. Do you not see my condition, and the condition of the people?
Go back to your family. And when you hear that I have prevailed, come to me." <!-- c09-007 -->

## s04 -- A Traveller from Ghifar
Image: desert/dunes-night.svg | pan-right | Desert dunes at night under a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Word of him was beginning to travel beyond Makkah. <!-- c09-008 -->

Abu Dharr, of the tribe of Ghifar, heard of a man in Makkah who said he was a prophet.
He sent his brother to find out the truth.
The brother came back and said: "I saw him enjoining good character, and speaking words that are not poetry."
Abu Dharr said: "You have not given me what I wanted." <!-- c09-008 -->

So he took his provisions and a waterskin, and went himself.
He came to the mosque and looked for the Prophet, but he did not know him, and he did not want to ask anyone.
When night came, Ali saw that he was a stranger, and took him home.
Neither of them asked the other about anything. <!-- c09-008 -->

On the third night, the narration says, Ali asked him what had brought him.
Abu Dharr made him promise to guide him, and then told him.
Ali said: "It is the truth. He is the Messenger of Allah. In the morning, follow me." <!-- c09-009 -->

He was taken to the Prophet, listened to him, and accepted Islam where he stood.
He was told to go back to his people until word came to him.
But first, the narration says, he went to the mosque and declared his faith aloud before Quraysh, and they beat him,
until al-Abbas shielded him and warned them that he was a man of Ghifar, whose land lay on their trade route to Syria. <!-- c09-009 -->

## s05 -- Who They Were
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Some of the first believers came from the leading families of Quraysh.
Many more were young, or poor, or without a clan to protect them, or were slaves. <!-- c09-010 -->

Years afterwards, the Roman emperor Heraclius questioned Abu Sufyan, who was then still an enemy of the Prophet, about this very matter.
He asked: "Do the nobles follow him, or the weak?"
Abu Sufyan answered: "The weak."
And Heraclius said: "Such are the followers of the messengers." <!-- c09-011 -->

He also asked whether any of them, once they had entered this religion, turned away from it out of displeasure with it.
Abu Sufyan answered: "No." <!-- c09-011 -->

In Makkah, that would soon be put to the test. <!-- n -->
