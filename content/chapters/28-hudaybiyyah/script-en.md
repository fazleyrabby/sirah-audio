# Hudaybiyyah

## s01 -- Toward the House
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

It had been six years since he left Makkah. <!-- c28-001 -->

In the sixth year after the Hijrah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ set out for Makkah with his Companions.
They did not go to fight.
They went as pilgrims, to visit the Ka'bah. <!-- c28-001 -->

Jabir, who was among them, said: "We were fourteen hundred." <!-- c28-002 -->

They put on the garments of pilgrimage, and drove their animals of sacrifice before them, garlanded, so that all could see why they had come. <!-- c28-001 -->

Quraysh sent out their cavalry to block the road.
So he turned aside, by a rough way through the hills, until he came to the edge of the sacred territory, at a place called Hudaybiyyah. <!-- c28-003 -->

## s02 -- The One Who Held Back the Elephant
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-left | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

There, his camel knelt, and would not rise.
The people said: "She has become stubborn."
He said: "She has not become stubborn. That is not her nature.
She has been held back by the One who held back the elephant." <!-- c28-004 -->

Then he said: "By the One in whose hand is my soul, they will not ask of me anything that honours what Allah has made sacred, except that I will grant it to them." <!-- c28-004 -->

Envoys began to go back and forth.
To the first of them he said:
"We have not come to fight anyone. We have come to visit the House.
War has worn Quraysh down.
If they wish, I will set a term of peace between us." <!-- c28-005 -->

One of the envoys of Quraysh, Urwah ibn Mas'ud, went back and told them what he had seen.
"I have been to the courts of kings: to Caesar, to Chosroes, to the Negus.
By Allah, I have never seen a king revered by his companions as Muhammad is revered by his.
He has offered you a sound proposal. Accept it." <!-- c28-006 -->

## s03 -- The Pledge Under the Tree
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The historians of the Seerah report that he sent Uthman ibn Affan into Makkah to speak to Quraysh,
and that Uthman was held there so long that word spread that he had been killed. <!-- c28-007 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ called his Companions to pledge themselves to him.
They came to him, one after another, as he sat beneath a tree. <!-- c28-008 -->

The Qur'an speaks of that pledge.
Its meaning is:
"Allah was well pleased with the believers when they pledged themselves to you under the tree.
He knew what was in their hearts.
So He sent down calm upon them, and rewarded them with a victory near at hand." <!-- c28-009 -->

Jabir said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told them that day: "You are the best of the people of the earth." <!-- c28-002 -->

Then Uthman returned, unharmed. <!-- c28-007 -->

## s04 -- The Treaty
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Quraysh sent a man named Suhayl ibn Amr to make terms.
When he saw him coming, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Your matter has been made easy for you." <!-- c28-010 -->

He called his scribe, and said: "Write: In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."
Suhayl said: "As for 'the Most Gracious', by Allah, I do not know what that is. Write: In Your name, O Allah."
The Muslims said: "By Allah, we will write nothing but: In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."
The Messenger of Allah said: "Write: In Your name, O Allah." <!-- c28-010 -->

Then he said: "This is what Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, has agreed."
Suhayl said: "By Allah, if we knew that you were the Messenger of Allah, we would not have barred you from the House, nor fought you. Write: Muhammad, son of Abdullah."
He said: "By Allah, I am the Messenger of Allah, even if you deny me.
Write: Muhammad, son of Abdullah." <!-- c28-010 -->

The terms were hard.
The Muslims would go back this year without visiting the House, and return the next.
And any man of Quraysh who came to the Muslims would be sent back to Quraysh, even if he had accepted Islam. <!-- c28-011 -->

## s05 -- Abu Jandal
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-right | A valley at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

While the document was still being written, a man staggered into the Muslim camp, dragging his chains.
He was Abu Jandal, a Muslim.
And he was the son of Suhayl. <!-- c28-012 -->

Suhayl said: "Muhammad, this is the first man whose return I demand of you."
Abu Jandal cried out: "Muslims! Am I to be sent back to the idolaters, when I have come to you as a Muslim?
Do you not see what I have suffered?" <!-- c28-012 -->

The Companions were shaken.
Umar went to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and said: "Are you not truly the Prophet of Allah?"
He said: "Yes."
Umar said: "Are we not upon the truth, and our enemy upon falsehood?"
He said: "Yes."
Umar said: "Then why should we accept humiliation in our religion?" <!-- c28-013 -->

He said: "I am the Messenger of Allah.
I do not disobey Him.
And He is my helper." <!-- c28-013 -->

## s06 -- A Clear Victory
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

When the treaty was written, he told his Companions: "Rise, make your sacrifices, and shave your heads."
Not a man among them rose.
He said it three times. <!-- c28-014 -->

He went in to his wife Umm Salamah, and told her what he had met with from the people.
She said: "Prophet of Allah, do you wish them to do it?
Then go out, and say not a word to any of them, until you have made your own sacrifice, and called your barber to shave you." <!-- c28-014 -->

He went out and did so.
And when they saw it, they rose. <!-- c28-014 -->

On the road back to Madinah, a chapter of the Qur'an was revealed.
Its opening means: "We have given you a clear victory." <!-- c28-015 -->

It did not look like a victory.
But al-Bara said, years later:
"You count the conquest of Makkah as the victory.
We count the victory as the pledge of Ridwan, on the day of Hudaybiyyah." <!-- c28-016 -->

For the first time, the two sides were at peace, and people could meet and speak freely.
The historians report that in the two years that followed, more people entered Islam than in all the years before. <!-- c28-017 -->
