# The Conquest of Makkah

## s01 -- The Treaty Broken
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-left | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The peace of Hudaybiyyah held for less than two years. <!-- c31-001 -->

The historians of the Seerah report how it ended.
A tribe allied to Quraysh attacked a tribe allied to the Muslims, by night, at a watering place.
And men of Quraysh helped them, with weapons, and some with their own hands. <!-- c31-001 -->

The treaty had been broken, and by those who had dictated its terms. <!-- c31-001 -->

## s02 -- Ten Thousand
Image: desert/dunes-night.svg | slow-zoom | Desert dunes at night under a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

In the month of Ramadan, in the eighth year after the Hijrah, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ set out from Madinah.
His Companion Ibn Abbas said that there were ten thousand with him. <!-- c31-002 -->

He kept their destination hidden, so that Quraysh would have no time to prepare, and there would be no battle in the sacred town. <!-- c31-003 -->

They made camp at a place a short march from Makkah.
That night, by his order, every man lit a fire. <!-- c31-004 -->

Abu Sufyan had come out with two others to look for news.
They saw the plain covered in fires.
Abu Sufyan said: "What is this? They are like the fires of Arafah." <!-- c31-004 -->

The sentries found them, and brought them to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.
And there, Abu Sufyan accepted Islam. <!-- c31-004 -->

## s03 -- The Day the Ka'bah Is Exalted
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-right | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

In the morning, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told his uncle al-Abbas to keep Abu Sufyan at the narrow part of the valley, so that he could watch the army pass. <!-- c31-005 -->

Tribe after tribe went by, each under its banner.
Then came a force unlike any he had seen: the Ansar, with Sa'd ibn Ubadah carrying their banner. <!-- c31-005 -->

As he passed, Sa'd called out: "Abu Sufyan! Today is the day of slaughter.
Today the sanctity of the Ka'bah will be set aside." <!-- c31-005 -->

When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came by, in the smallest company of all, Abu Sufyan told him what Sa'd had said.
He answered: "Sa'd has spoken wrongly.
This is a day on which Allah will exalt the Ka'bah.
A day on which the Ka'bah will be clothed." <!-- c31-005 -->

And he gave his word to the people of Makkah.
"Whoever enters the house of Abu Sufyan is safe.
Whoever lays down his weapons is safe.
Whoever shuts his door is safe." <!-- c31-006 -->

## s04 -- Entering Makkah
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He entered Makkah from its upper side.
It was eight years since he had left it by night. <!-- c31-007 -->

One of his Companions said: "I saw the Messenger of Allah on the day of the conquest, on his camel, reciting the chapter of Victory." <!-- c31-008 -->

The historians report that he rode in with his head bowed, in humility before Allah, until his beard nearly touched the saddle. <!-- c31-009 -->

There was almost no fighting. <!-- c31-007 -->

## s05 -- Truth Has Come
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He went to the Ka'bah. <!-- c31-010 -->

Ibn Mas'ud said that there were three hundred and sixty idols around the House.
He began to strike them with a stick that was in his hand, saying:
"Truth has come, and falsehood has vanished.
Truth has come, and falsehood neither begins anything, nor brings it back." <!-- c31-010 -->

He would not enter the Ka'bah until the images inside it had been taken out.
Then he went in, and proclaimed the greatness of Allah in every corner of it. <!-- c31-011 -->

The House that Ibrahim and Ismail had raised for the worship of Allah alone was again what it had been built to be. <!-- c31-011 -->

## s06 -- The People of Makkah
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.



A famous exchange is reported here, in which he asked them what they expected of him, and told them: "Go, for you are free."
Its chain of narration is not strong, and we do not relate it as his words. <!-- c31-012 -->

What is established is what he did.
He had given them safety before he entered, and he kept it.
There was no vengeance, and no plunder.
And from that day, those people of Makkah were known as the freed ones. <!-- c31-013 -->

The next day, he stood and addressed them.
"It is Allah who made Makkah sacred, not the people.
It is not lawful for anyone who believes in Allah and the Last Day to shed blood in it, or to cut down a tree in it.
It was made lawful to me only for an hour of one day.
And today its sanctity has returned, as it was yesterday.
Let the one who is present tell the one who is absent." <!-- c31-014 -->

A chapter of the Qur'an speaks of that time.
Its meaning is:
"When the help of Allah comes, and the victory,
and you see the people entering the religion of Allah in crowds,
then glorify the praise of your Lord, and ask His forgiveness.
He is ever accepting of repentance." <!-- c31-015 -->
