# The Journey

## s01 -- The Coast Road
Image: maps/arabia.svg | static | A map of the Arabian peninsula with Makkah and Yathrib marked and the Red Sea coast between them. | 0 | 24% 50%
Audio: Voice only.

The usual road from Makkah to Yathrib ran north through the hills.
The guide did not take it.
He led them west, toward the sea, and then north along the coast, by tracks that few travellers used. <!-- c21-001 -->

There were four of them: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, Abu Bakr, his freed slave Amir ibn Fuhayrah, and the guide. <!-- c21-001 -->

## s02 -- The Shade of a Rock
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Abu Bakr later told the story of that first stretch to a man of Madinah, who had asked him for it. <!-- c21-002 -->

"We travelled through the night," he said, "and the next day, until it was noon.
The road was empty. No one was passing.
Then a long rock rose before us, with shade beneath it that the sun had not yet reached." <!-- c21-002 -->

"We dismounted there.
I levelled a place with my hands for the Prophet to sleep on, in the shade, and spread a fur over it.
I said: Sleep, Messenger of Allah, and I will keep watch around you.
So he slept." <!-- c21-002 -->

While he watched, Abu Bakr saw a shepherd coming toward the rock with his flock, looking for the same shade.
He asked whether the sheep had milk, and whether he would milk some for them.
The shepherd agreed. <!-- c21-002 -->

Abu Bakr had him wipe the dust from the udder, and from his hands.
Then he poured a little water over the vessel, until the milk was cool.
He carried it to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and found him awake.
"I said: Drink, Messenger of Allah.
And he drank, until I was content." <!-- c21-002 -->

## s03 -- Suraqah
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | pan-left | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

One man did find them.
His name was Suraqah ibn Malik, of the tribe of Mudlij, and he told the story himself. <!-- c21-003 -->

He was sitting among his people when a man came and said that he had seen figures far off, along the coast.
Suraqah knew at once who they must be.
But he said: "No, those are not them."
He wanted the reward for himself. <!-- c21-003 -->

He waited a while, then went home, had his horse brought out behind a hill, took his spear, and left by the back of his house. <!-- c21-003 -->

As he came near them, his horse stumbled, and threw him.
He drew lots with his divining arrows, to see whether he should harm them or not.
The answer he did not want came out.
He rode on anyway. <!-- c21-003 -->

He came close enough to hear the Messenger of Allah ﷺ reciting the Qur'an.
"He did not turn to look," Suraqah said, "while Abu Bakr kept looking back." <!-- c21-004 -->

Then the forelegs of his horse sank into the ground, up to its knees, and he fell.
He drew lots again.
Again, the answer he did not want. <!-- c21-004 -->

## s04 -- A Writing of Safety
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

So Suraqah called out to them that they were safe from him. <!-- c21-005 -->

"When I saw how I had been held back from them," he said, "it came to my heart that the cause of the Messenger of Allah would prevail." <!-- c21-005 -->

He told them of the reward on their heads, and of what Quraysh intended.
He offered them provisions and gear.
They took nothing from him, and asked nothing of him, except this.
The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Keep our matter hidden." <!-- c21-005 -->

Suraqah asked for a written guarantee of safety.
Amir ibn Fuhayrah wrote it for him, on a piece of leather.
And they rode on. <!-- c21-005 -->

Another Companion, Anas, put it in a sentence:
"At the beginning of the day, he was striving against the Prophet of Allah.
At the end of it, he was a guard for him." <!-- c21-006 -->

## s05 -- A Man Who Shows Me the Way
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Abu Bakr had travelled this country as a merchant, and people along the road knew his face.
They did not know the man riding with him. <!-- c21-007 -->

Anas narrated that someone would meet Abu Bakr and ask: "Who is this man in front of you?"
And he would answer: "This man shows me the way."
The questioner would take him to mean the road.
Abu Bakr meant the way of what is good. <!-- c21-007 -->

Somewhere on that road, they met a caravan of Muslim merchants returning from Syria.
Among them was az-Zubayr.
He gave the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and Abu Bakr white garments to wear. <!-- c21-008 -->

So it was in white that the people of Yathrib would first see them, coming out of the desert haze. <!-- c21-008 -->
