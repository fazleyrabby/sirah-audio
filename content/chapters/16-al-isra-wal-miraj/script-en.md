# Al-Isra' wal-Mi'raj

## s01 -- One Night
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | slow-zoom | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

In the years between Ta'if and the Hijrah, there was a night unlike any other in his life. <!-- c16-003 -->

The Qur'an speaks of it.
Its meaning is:
"Glory be to Him who took His servant by night, from the Sacred Mosque to the Farthest Mosque, whose surroundings We have blessed, to show him some of Our signs." <!-- c16-001 -->

The journey by night to Jerusalem is called al-Isra'.
The ascent from there through the heavens is called al-Mi'raj. <!-- c16-002 -->

When exactly it took place is not agreed upon.
The scholars have given many dates.
What they agree on is that it was in Makkah, before the Hijrah. <!-- c16-003 -->

## s02 -- To the Farthest Mosque
Image: desert/dunes-night.svg | pan-right | Desert dunes at night under a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The reports record the account he gave of that night. <!-- c16-004 c16-005 -->

He said that al-Buraq was brought to him: a white animal, larger than a donkey and smaller than a mule, which placed its step at the farthest reach of its sight. <!-- c16-004 -->

"I rode it," he said, "until I came to Jerusalem.
I tied it to the ring that the prophets used.
Then I entered the mosque, and prayed two units of prayer in it." <!-- c16-004 -->

"Then I came out, and Jibril brought me a vessel of wine and a vessel of milk.
I chose the milk.
And Jibril said: You have chosen the natural way." <!-- c16-004 -->

## s03 -- The Ascent
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars. No figures and nothing of the unseen.
Audio: Voice only.

Then, he said, he was taken up. <!-- c16-005 -->

At the nearest heaven, Jibril asked for the gate to be opened.
It was asked: "Who is it?"
He said: "Jibril."
"And who is with you?"
"Muhammad."
"Has he been sent for?"
"Yes."
And it was said: "Welcome to him. What an excellent arrival is his." <!-- c16-005 -->

In that heaven he met Adam, who greeted him: "Welcome to the righteous son, and the righteous prophet." <!-- c16-005 -->

So it was at each heaven: the question at the gate, and the welcome.
In the second, he met Yahya and Isa.
In the third, Yusuf.
In the fourth, Idris.
In the fifth, Harun.
In the sixth, Musa.
And in the seventh, Ibrahim. <!-- c16-005 -->

Then he was raised to the Lote Tree of the Farthest Boundary. <!-- c16-005 -->
The Qur'an says of that place:
"The sight did not swerve, nor did it overstep.
He saw some of the greatest signs of his Lord." <!-- c16-006 -->

## s04 -- The Prayer
Image: mountains/hira-night.svg | slow-zoom | A bare mountain under a night sky with a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

There, he said, fifty prayers in every day and night were made obligatory upon his community. <!-- c16-007 -->

On his way back he passed Musa, who asked what had been enjoined on him.
He told him.
Musa said: "Your community will not be able to bear fifty prayers a day.
I have tested people before you, and I struggled hard with the Children of Israel.
Go back to your Lord, and ask Him to lighten it for your community." <!-- c16-007 -->

He went back, and it was reduced.
He came again to Musa, and Musa said the same.
Again and again he returned, until the prayers were five in every day and night. <!-- c16-007 -->

Musa told him to go back once more.
He said: "I have asked my Lord until I am ashamed.
I am content, and I submit." <!-- c16-007 -->

The five daily prayers are the one obligation that was given to him on that night, above the heavens. <!-- c16-007 -->

## s05 -- Morning in Makkah
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

By morning, he was in Makkah again.
And he told the people. <!-- c16-008 -->

Quraysh did not believe him.
A caravan took a month to reach Jerusalem, and a month to return. <!-- c16-008 -->

He said: "When Quraysh disbelieved me, I stood in the enclosure beside the Ka'bah.
And Allah displayed Jerusalem before me.
I began to describe its features to them, while I was looking at it." <!-- c16-009 -->

The historians report that some people went to Abu Bakr with the news, expecting him to waver.
It is reported that he answered: "If he said it, then it is true.
I believe him in what is greater than that: in news that comes to him from heaven."
And they report that from that day he was called as-Siddiq: the one who affirms the truth. <!-- c16-010 -->
