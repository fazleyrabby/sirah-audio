# Migration to Abyssinia

## s01 -- A Land Across the Sea
Image: maps/arabia.svg | static | A map of the Arabian peninsula with Makkah marked and Abyssinia across the Red Sea. | 0 | 24% 50%
Audio: Voice only.

Across the Red Sea from Arabia lay the Christian kingdom of Abyssinia.
Its king was known to the Arabs by his title: the Negus. <!-- c12-001 -->

The historians of the Seerah report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told his Companions to go there.
They report that he described it as a land of truth, whose king wronged no one. <!-- c12-002 -->

In the fifth year of his mission, they report, a first small group slipped out of Makkah and took ship.
Among them were Uthman ibn Affan and his wife Ruqayyah, the daughter of the Messenger of Allah. <!-- c12-003 -->

A larger group followed, more than eighty men, and women with them.
Among them was Ja'far, the son of Abu Talib. <!-- c12-004 -->

## s02 -- The Envoys of Quraysh
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-left | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

What followed was narrated by one of the emigrants, Umm Salamah, who later became a wife of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.
Her account is recorded in the Musnad of Imam Ahmad. <!-- c12-005 -->

She said that Quraysh would not leave them in peace even there.
They sent two envoys to the Negus, carrying gifts for him and for his generals.
The envoys asked that the emigrants be handed over, without being heard. <!-- c12-005 -->

The Negus refused.
He would not surrender people who had sought his protection, she said, until he had called them and asked them himself. <!-- c12-005 -->

## s03 -- Ja'far Speaks
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The one who spoke for the emigrants was Ja'far. <!-- c12-006 -->

Umm Salamah reported what he told the king.
He said that they had been a people of ignorance.
They worshipped idols, ate carrion, committed shameful deeds, cut the ties of kinship, and treated their neighbours badly.
The strong among them devoured the weak. <!-- c12-006 -->

Then Allah sent them a messenger from among themselves, a man whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness, and decency they already knew.
He called them to worship Allah alone, and to leave the stones and idols their fathers had worshipped.
He told them to speak the truth, to keep their trusts, to honour the ties of kinship, to be good to their neighbours, and to hold back from bloodshed. <!-- c12-006 -->

For this, Ja'far said, their people had attacked them and tormented them.
And so they had come to his land, and chosen him over others, hoping not to be wronged under him. <!-- c12-006 -->

## s04 -- The Answer of the Negus
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The Negus asked whether Ja'far had with him anything of what this messenger had brought from Allah.
Ja'far recited to him from the opening of the chapter of Maryam. <!-- c12-007 -->

Umm Salamah said that the Negus wept until his beard was wet, and his bishops wept with him.
Then he said that this, and what Musa had brought, came from the same lamp.
He told the envoys of Quraysh to go.
He would not hand these people over. <!-- c12-007 -->

The envoys tried once more.
They told the king that the emigrants said a grave thing about Isa, the son of Maryam.
So he sent for them again, and asked them. <!-- c12-008 -->

Ja'far answered with what their Prophet had taught them: that Isa is the servant of Allah, His messenger, His spirit, and His word, which He cast to the virgin Maryam. <!-- c12-008 -->
The Negus picked up a stick from the ground.
He said that Isa, the son of Maryam, did not exceed what Ja'far had said by the length of that stick. <!-- c12-008 -->

The gifts were returned to the envoys.
And the emigrants, Umm Salamah said, lived there in the best of homes, with the best of neighbours. <!-- c12-008 -->

## s05 -- Hamzah and Umar
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

In Makkah, meanwhile, the believers gained two men whom Quraysh feared. <!-- c12-009 c12-012 -->

The first, the historians report, was Hamzah, the uncle of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. <!-- c12-009 -->

The second was Umar, the son of al-Khattab.
The Messenger of Allah had prayed: "O Allah, strengthen Islam with whichever of these two men is more beloved to You: Abu Jahl, or Umar ibn al-Khattab."
And the more beloved of them to Him was Umar. <!-- c12-010 -->

Several accounts are told of how Umar came to accept Islam.
Their chains of narration are not strong, and so we do not choose among them here. <!-- c12-011 -->

What is established is what his son Abdullah saw as a boy, from the roof of their house.
A crowd had gathered, saying: "Umar has left his religion."
Then a man in a silk cloak came, and said: "Umar has left his religion. What of it? I am his protector."
And the people went away. <!-- c12-012 -->

Ibn Mas'ud said: "We have been strong ever since Umar accepted Islam." <!-- c12-013 -->

## s06 -- Those Who Stayed
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Some of the emigrants returned to Makkah in the years that followed.
Others, Ja'far among them, stayed in Abyssinia for many years, and came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ only after he had settled in Madinah. <!-- c12-014 -->

The Negus himself did not live to meet him.
On the day he died, the Messenger of Allah told his Companions, far away in Madinah.
He said: "Today a righteous man has died. So rise, and pray for your brother." <!-- c12-015 -->

And he led them in the funeral prayer for a king who had sheltered them when no one else would. <!-- c12-015 -->
