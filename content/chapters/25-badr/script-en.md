# Badr

## s01 -- The Caravan
Image: maps/arabia.svg | static | A map of the Arabian peninsula showing the caravan route between Makkah and Syria passing near Madinah. | 0 | 24% 50%
Audio: Voice only.

In the month of Ramadan, in the second year after the Hijrah, word reached Madinah of a great caravan of Quraysh.
It was returning from Syria, laden with goods, under the command of Abu Sufyan. <!-- c25-001 -->

Much of the wealth the Emigrants had left behind in Makkah had been seized by the very men whose goods that caravan carried. <!-- c25-001 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ set out to meet it.
His Companion Ka'b ibn Malik said that he went out seeking only the caravan of Quraysh,
until Allah brought them and their enemy together without any appointment. <!-- c25-002 -->

For Abu Sufyan learned of the danger.
The historians of the Seerah report that he turned his caravan aside toward the coast, and sent a rider to Makkah for help.
And Makkah sent an army. <!-- c25-003 -->

## s02 -- The Counsel
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-right | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The men who had come out with him had come for a caravan, not for a battle.
So he asked them what they thought. <!-- c25-004 -->

One of the Emigrants, al-Miqdad, stood and spoke.
His Companion Ibn Mas'ud said that he would rather have been the one who said those words than have anything else in the world. <!-- c25-005 -->

Al-Miqdad said: "We will not say to you what the people of Musa said to him: Go, you and your Lord, and fight.
We will fight on your right, and on your left, and in front of you, and behind you." <!-- c25-005 -->

Ibn Mas'ud said: "I saw the face of the Prophet shine, and it gladdened him." <!-- c25-005 -->

Then the Ansar spoke, through one of their leaders.
They had pledged to protect him in Madinah, and this was not Madinah.
He told the Messenger of Allah that if he asked them to ride into the sea, they would ride into it with him. <!-- c25-004 -->

## s03 -- Three Hundred
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The two forces met at the wells of Badr, south-west of Madinah. <!-- c25-006 -->

Al-Bara said: "We, the Companions of Muhammad, used to say that the number of those at Badr was the number of the companions of Talut who crossed the river with him: a little over three hundred and ten." <!-- c25-007 -->

Umar said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ looked at the idolaters, and they were a thousand.
And his own Companions were three hundred and nineteen. <!-- c25-008 -->

The historians report that the Muslims had only two horses among them, and seventy camels, which they rode by turns. <!-- c25-009 -->

## s04 -- From Night to Day
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The Qur'an recalls the night before the battle.
Its meaning is:
"He covered you with drowsiness, as a security from Him.
And He sent down upon you water from the sky, to purify you, and to make firm your feet." <!-- c25-010 -->

On the day of Badr, Umar described what the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did.
He turned to face the direction of prayer, stretched out his hands, and called upon his Lord.
"O Allah, fulfil for me what You have promised me.
O Allah, if this small band of the people of Islam is destroyed, You will not be worshipped on the earth." <!-- c25-008 -->

He went on calling, his hands stretched out, until his cloak slipped from his shoulders.
Abu Bakr came, picked up the cloak, and put it back on him.
Then he held him from behind, and said:
"Prophet of Allah, this pleading with your Lord is enough.
He will fulfil for you what He has promised you." <!-- c25-008 -->

And a verse was revealed.
Its meaning is:
"When you asked your Lord for help, and He answered you: I will reinforce you with a thousand of the angels, following one upon another." <!-- c25-011 -->

## s05 -- The Day of Badr
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-left | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The battle was fought, the historians report, on the seventeenth of Ramadan. <!-- c25-012 -->

By the end of the day, the army of Quraysh had broken.
Al-Bara said that seventy of them were killed, and seventy were taken captive. <!-- c25-013 -->

Among the dead were many of the leaders of Makkah, the men who had led the persecution there.
Abu Jahl was one of them. <!-- c25-014 -->
Umayyah ibn Khalaf, who had been the master of Bilal, was another. <!-- c25-015 -->

Of the Muslims, the historians report, fourteen were killed. <!-- c25-016 -->

The Qur'an says of that day:
"Allah gave you victory at Badr, when you were few and weak.
So be mindful of Allah, that you may be grateful." <!-- c25-017 -->

## s06 -- The Captives
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Seventy men of Quraysh were brought back to Madinah as captives. <!-- c25-013 -->

The historians report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told his Companions to treat them well,
and that some of the captives later said their captors had given them bread, while eating only dates themselves. <!-- c25-018 -->

Most were released for a ransom.
And it is reported that some who had no wealth, but who could read and write, were set free in return for teaching the children of the Ansar. <!-- c25-019 -->

Those who had fought at Badr were honoured for the rest of their lives.
Years later, when one of them made a grave mistake, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"He was at Badr.
And what do you know? Perhaps Allah looked upon the people of Badr, and said: Do as you wish, for I have forgiven you." <!-- c25-020 -->
