# Khadijah رضي الله عنها

## s01 -- The Merchant
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Khadijah, the daughter of Khuwaylid, was a woman of Quraysh, of standing and of wealth.
The historians report that she was a merchant, who hired men to carry her goods to market in return for a share of the profit. <!-- c06-001 -->

When she heard of the truthfulness and trustworthiness of Muhammad ﷺ, she sent to him.
She offered him her goods to take to Syria, and she sent her servant, Maysarah, with him. <!-- c06-002 -->

He returned with a good profit.
And Maysarah, the Seerah reports, told her what he had seen of the young man's conduct on the road. <!-- c06-003 -->

## s02 -- The Marriage
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Khadijah then proposed marriage to him.
The historians report that she did so through a friend, Nafisah.
He spoke to his uncles, and the marriage was concluded. <!-- c06-004 -->

He was twenty-five years old.
Her age is most commonly reported as forty, though other reports say she was younger. <!-- c06-005 -->

As long as she lived, he did not marry another woman. <!-- c06-006 -->

## s03 -- The Household
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

All of his children but one were born to Khadijah.
The Seerah names them: al-Qasim, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, Fatimah, and Abdullah.
His sons died while they were still small. <!-- c06-007 -->

Long afterwards, speaking of Khadijah, he said: "From her I had children." <!-- c06-008 -->

Others were raised in that house as well.
There was Zayd, the son of Harithah, who had come to it as a servant.
He was set free, and brought up as a son.
People called him Zayd, son of Muhammad, until the Qur'an commanded that those who are taken in should be called by the names of their own fathers. <!-- c06-009 -->

And there was Ali, the young son of Abu Talib.
The historians report that he was taken into the household to lighten the burden on his father. <!-- c06-010 -->

## s04 -- The Black Stone
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-right | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

When he was thirty-five, the historians report, Quraysh rebuilt the Ka'bah, after a flood had damaged its walls. <!-- c06-011 -->

He carried stones with the others.
His Companion Jabir narrated that his uncle al-Abbas told him to take off his waist-cloth and put it on his shoulder, to protect it from the stones.
When he did, he fell to the ground.
He covered himself again, and he was never seen uncovered after that. <!-- c06-012 -->

When the walls reached the place of the Black Stone, the Seerah reports, the clans quarrelled over who would have the honour of setting it,
and they came close to bloodshed.
They agreed to let the next man who came through the gate decide.
It was Muhammad.
They said: "This is the Trustworthy. We accept him." <!-- c06-013 -->

He asked for a cloak, and placed the stone upon it.
He had the leader of each clan take hold of an edge, and they lifted it together.
Then he set the stone in its place with his own hands. <!-- c06-013 -->

## s05 -- What She Was to Him
Image: desert/dunes-night.svg | slow-zoom | Desert dunes at night under a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Years after Khadijah's death, his wife Aishah said that she was never as jealous of any of his wives as she was of Khadijah, whom she had never seen.
He would mention her often.
And when he slaughtered a sheep, he would send portions of it to Khadijah's friends. <!-- c06-014 -->

He told of how the angel Jibril once came to him and said that Khadijah was approaching with food.
"When she comes to you, give her greetings of peace from her Lord,
and give her glad tidings of a house in Paradise, where there is no noise, and no weariness." <!-- c06-015 -->

And he said: "The best of the women of her time was Maryam, and the best of the women of her time is Khadijah." <!-- c06-016 -->

Fifteen years after their marriage, he would come down from a mountain with his heart trembling.
And it was to her that he would go. <!-- c06-017 -->
