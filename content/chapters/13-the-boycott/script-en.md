# The Boycott

## s01 -- The Pact
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-left | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Persuasion had failed. Offers had failed. Torment had failed.
And the clan of Hashim still would not hand over their kinsman. <!-- c13-001 -->

So Quraysh turned against the whole clan.
The historians of the Seerah report that their leaders met, and bound themselves by a written pact against the clans of Hashim and al-Muttalib.
No one would marry among them.
No one would buy from them, or sell to them.
And this would hold until they gave up the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to be killed. <!-- c13-001 -->

They report that the document was hung inside the Ka'bah. <!-- c13-001 -->

## s02 -- The Ravine
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-right | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The two clans withdrew into a narrow valley on the edge of Makkah, known as the ravine of Abu Talib.
They went together, the historians report: those who believed, and those who did not.
They would not abandon their own. <!-- c13-002 -->

Only one man of the clan of Hashim stood with Quraysh.
That was Abu Lahab. <!-- c13-002 -->

It was, by most reports, the seventh year of the mission.
The boycott lasted between two and three years. <!-- c13-003 -->

## s03 -- Hunger
Image: desert/dunes-night.svg | slow-zoom | Desert dunes at night under a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Food reached the ravine only in secret, or in the sacred months, when the people in it could come out to the markets.
Even then, the historians report, the merchants of Quraysh would bid up the prices against them, so that they went back with nothing. <!-- c13-004 -->

They report that the people were reduced to eating the leaves of trees,
and that the crying of hungry children could be heard from beyond the ravine. <!-- c13-004 -->

Some in Makkah could not bear it.
The historians name men who sent food in by night, among them Hakim ibn Hizam, a nephew of Khadijah,
and Hisham ibn Amr, who would load a camel, lead it to the mouth of the ravine in the dark, and let it go. <!-- c13-005 -->

Through all of it, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ went on calling people to Allah, whenever the sacred months allowed him to meet them. <!-- c13-005 -->

## s04 -- The Pact Undone
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

It was that same Hisham ibn Amr, the historians report, who set out to end it. <!-- c13-006 -->

He went to one man after another whose kinship with the clans in the ravine weighed on him.
Five of them agreed to stand up before Quraysh and declare that they would no longer be bound by the pact.
Among them was al-Mut'im ibn Adi. <!-- c13-006 -->

The Seerah also reports that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told his uncle Abu Talib that the document had been eaten by termites, all but the name of Allah,
and that when it was brought out, it was found to be so. <!-- c13-007 -->

The pact was torn up.
The clans came out of the ravine. <!-- c13-006 -->

## s05 -- The Place Remembered
Image: desert/dunes-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | Empty desert at dawn, light spreading over the dunes. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The details of those years come to us from the historians.
But the pact itself is confirmed in the words of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. <!-- c13-008 -->

Many years later, he returned to Makkah at the head of the pilgrims.
His Companion Abu Hurayrah narrated that on the day of sacrifice, at Mina, he said:
"Tomorrow, we shall camp at the valley of the sons of Kinanah, where they swore their oath of disbelief." <!-- c13-008 -->

He meant the place where Quraysh and Kinanah had sworn against the sons of Hashim and the sons of al-Muttalib:
that they would not marry among them, nor trade with them, until they handed over the Prophet. <!-- c13-008 -->

He had not forgotten where it was done.
And he chose to camp there. <!-- c13-008 -->
