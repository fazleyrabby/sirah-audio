# The Call Becomes Public

## s01 -- The Command
Image: makkah/valley-dawn.svg | slow-zoom | First light over a valley town ringed by mountains. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Then the time of quiet came to an end. <!-- c10-001 c10-002 -->

Two commands were revealed.
The meaning of the first is: "Warn your nearest kindred." <!-- c10-001 -->
The meaning of the second is: "Proclaim what you are commanded, and turn away from those who set up partners with Allah." <!-- c10-002 -->

## s02 -- On the Hill of Safa
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-right | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

His Companion Ibn Abbas narrated what he did when the first of those verses came down. <!-- c10-003 -->

He climbed the hill of Safa, beside the Ka'bah, and called out to the clans of Quraysh by name.
They gathered to him.
Those who could not come sent someone to see what the matter was. <!-- c10-003 -->

He said to them: "Tell me. If I told you that horsemen were in the valley, about to attack you, would you believe me?"
They said: "Yes. We have never known you to say anything but the truth."
He said: "Then I am a warner to you, before a severe punishment." <!-- c10-003 -->

His uncle, Abu Lahab, answered him.
"May you perish for the rest of this day. Is this what you gathered us for?" <!-- c10-003 -->

And a chapter of the Qur'an was revealed.
The meaning of its opening is:
"May the hands of Abu Lahab perish, and may he perish.
His wealth will not avail him, nor what he has earned." <!-- c10-004 -->

## s03 -- I Cannot Avail You
Image: makkah/homes-night.svg | pan-left | Flat-roofed houses at night with a few lamp-lit windows. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

His Companion Abu Hurayrah narrated the words he spoke to his own family when that verse was revealed. <!-- c10-005 -->

"O people of Quraysh, ransom yourselves. I cannot avail you at all before Allah.
O sons of Abd Manaf, I cannot avail you at all before Allah.
O Safiyyah, aunt of the Messenger of Allah, I cannot avail you at all before Allah.
O Fatimah, daughter of Muhammad, ask me for whatever you wish of my wealth.
I cannot avail you at all before Allah." <!-- c10-005 -->

The warning was for everyone.
He began it with those he loved most. <!-- c10-003 c10-005 -->

## s04 -- What Quraysh Said
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-right | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The Qur'an records how the leaders of Makkah answered. <!-- c10-006 -->

Its meaning is:
"They wonder that a warner has come to them from among themselves.
And the disbelievers say: This is a sorcerer, a liar.
Has he made the gods into one God? This is a strange thing indeed." <!-- c10-006 -->

They called him a poet, and a soothsayer, and a man possessed. <!-- c10-007 -->
And they told one another not to listen.
The meaning of the verse is:
"Do not listen to this Qur'an. Make noise during it, so that you may prevail." <!-- c10-008 -->

They did not say that he was a man known for lying.
On the hill of Safa, they had said the opposite. <!-- c10-003 -->

## s05 -- Abu Talib
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

One thing stood between him and the anger of Quraysh: his uncle, Abu Talib. <!-- c10-009 -->

Abu Talib did not accept Islam.
But he was the head of the clan of Hashim, and he would not hand over his nephew.
His Companion Ibn Mas'ud said: "As for the Messenger of Allah, Allah protected him through his uncle, Abu Talib." <!-- c10-009 -->

The historians of the Seerah report that the leaders of Quraysh went to Abu Talib more than once,
asking him to restrain his nephew, or to stand aside. <!-- c10-010 -->

A well-known reply is attributed to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ on that occasion, about the sun and the moon being placed in his hands.
Its chain of narration has been graded weak, and so we do not relate it as his words. <!-- c10-011 -->

What is established is the outcome.
Abu Talib did not give him up, and the call did not stop. <!-- c10-010 -->

## s06 -- An Offer
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The historians also report that Quraysh tried another way. <!-- c10-012 -->

They report that one of their leaders, Utbah ibn Rabi'ah, came to him with an offer.
If he wanted wealth, they would make him the richest of them.
If he wanted honour, they would make him their chief.
If he wanted kingship, they would make him king. <!-- c10-012 -->

When Utbah had finished, the report says, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ recited to him from the Qur'an, from the chapter called Fussilat.
And Utbah went back to his people and told them to leave this man alone. <!-- c10-012 -->

The next chapter turns to persecution in Makkah. <!-- n -->
