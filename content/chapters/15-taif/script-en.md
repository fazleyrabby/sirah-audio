# Ta'if

## s01 -- The Road East
Image: mountains/ridges-dusk.svg | pan-right | Layered mountain ridges at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

Ta'if lies in the hills to the east of Makkah, some two days' journey away.
It was the home of the tribe of Thaqif: a town of gardens and vineyards, cooler and greener than Makkah. <!-- c15-001 -->

The historians of the Seerah report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ went there in the tenth year of his mission, not long after the death of Abu Talib.
With him was Zayd, the son of Harithah. <!-- c15-002 -->

He went to ask Thaqif for what Quraysh had refused him: a hearing, and protection. <!-- c15-002 -->

## s02 -- Refused
Image: desert/dunes-day.svg | pan-left | Empty desert dunes under a pale sky. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The historians report that he went to the leaders of Thaqif, three brothers, and spoke to them.
They refused him, and they mocked him.
He asked them, at least, to keep his visit quiet.
They did not grant him even that. <!-- c15-003 -->

They report that the leaders set the rabble of the town on him.
They lined his path, shouting, and threw stones, until his feet bled.
Zayd tried to shield him with his own body, and was wounded in the head. <!-- c15-004 -->

The two of them took shelter at the wall of an orchard outside the town. <!-- c15-004 -->

## s03 -- At the Orchard
Image: desert/oasis-dusk.svg | slow-zoom | Palm trees at an oasis at dusk. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

A supplication is widely attributed to him at that orchard, beginning with the words: "O Allah, to You I complain of my weakness."
Its chain of narration has been graded weak, and so we do not relate it as his words. <!-- c15-005 -->

The historians also report that the owners of the orchard, two men of Quraysh, saw his condition,
and sent their servant to him with a dish of grapes.
The servant was a Christian named Addas, from Nineveh. <!-- c15-006 -->

When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ put out his hand to eat, he said: "In the name of Allah."
Addas said that the people of this land did not speak such words.
He asked him where he was from, and Addas told him.
The report says that he replied: "From the town of the righteous man, Yunus, the son of Matta."
And when Addas asked how he knew of Yunus, he said: "He is my brother. He was a prophet, and I am a prophet." <!-- c15-006 -->

## s04 -- The Hardest Day
Image: mountains/hira-night.svg | pan-left | A bare mountain under a night sky with a thin moon. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

What happened next, he told himself. <!-- c15-007 -->

Years later, his wife Aishah asked him whether any day had been harder on him than the day of Uhud.
He said: "I suffered from your people what I suffered.
And the hardest of it was on the day of Aqabah, when I offered myself to Ibn Abd Yalil, and he did not answer me as I had hoped." <!-- c15-007 -->

"So I left, overcome with grief, and I did not come to myself until I was at Qarn ath-Tha'alib.
I raised my head, and there was a cloud shading me.
I looked, and in it was Jibril." <!-- c15-007 -->

"He called to me, and said: Allah has heard what your people said to you, and how they answered you.
He has sent you the angel of the mountains, so that you may command him as you wish concerning them." <!-- c15-007 -->

"Then the angel of the mountains called to me, and greeted me, and said:
O Muhammad, it is as you wish.
If you wish, I will bring the two mountains together upon them." <!-- c15-007 -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"No. I hope that Allah will bring out from their descendants people who worship Allah alone, and set nothing beside Him." <!-- c15-007 -->

## s05 -- Listeners in the Night
Image: desert/night-sky.svg | slow-zoom | A wide night sky full of stars above a dark ridge. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

The Qur'an tells of another audience, whom he did not see.
Its meaning is:
"And when We turned toward you a company of the jinn, listening to the Qur'an.
When they came into its presence, they said: Be silent.
And when it was finished, they went back to their people as warners." <!-- c15-008 -->

The historians place this on his way back from Ta'if, at a place called Nakhlah, as he stood reciting in prayer at night. <!-- c15-009 -->

## s06 -- Return to Makkah
Image: makkah/valley-night.svg | pan-right | A valley town at night beneath the stars. No figures.
Audio: Voice only.

He could not simply walk back into Makkah.
He had gone to another tribe for help, and Quraysh knew it. <!-- c15-010 -->

The historians report that he sent word to several men of Makkah, asking for protection.
The one who gave it was al-Mut'im ibn Adi, who was not a Muslim.
He armed himself and his sons, went out to the Ka'bah, and announced that Muhammad was under his protection. <!-- c15-010 -->

Al-Mut'im died before he could accept Islam.
But years afterwards, when prisoners of Quraysh were in his hands after the battle of Badr, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:
"If al-Mut'im ibn Adi were alive, and spoke to me about these men, I would have let them go for his sake." <!-- c15-011 -->

At Ta'if he declined to ask for the town's destruction. Years later, he said he would have released the captives of Badr for al-Mut'im's sake. <!-- c15-007 c15-011 -->
