# Landscape artwork direction — review draft

Use these prompts for a small audition before replacing the existing chapter visuals. No chapter script text is included. All scenes depict the environment only, with no people, named historical figures, animals representing the unseen, Arabic calligraphy, or modern objects. Each result must be inspected before it enters `public/images/` and then recorded in a visual manifest with its prompt, tool, date, and human reviewer.

## Shared look

Natural documentary photography inspired by the Hijaz landscape; restrained contrast, muted sandstone and warm earth, soft atmospheric haze, believable rock geology and building materials, generous quiet space for the listening interface. Calm rather than dramatic. No halos, magical light, battle action, or fantasy architecture. Artistic reconstruction, never presented as a photograph of a historical event.

## Three audition frames

1. **Mount Hira before dawn.** A distant, rugged granite mountain above a broad empty valley. Blue-grey pre-dawn sky, a fine trace of warm light at the horizon, physically plausible stone texture, very subtle atmospheric haze. The cave may be suggested as a small dark opening only if its placement is credible. No people, faces, clothing, figures, supernatural forms, inscriptions, or modern buildings.
2. **An empty Hijaz road at dawn.** A quiet stony track through low desert ridges; sparse scrub, natural sand and stone, long soft morning shadows, dust only as atmospheric haze. No travellers, footprints, caravans, animals, vehicles, or text.
3. **A distant early Arabian town at dusk.** Low, simple mud-brick and stone structures in a mountain valley, viewed from far enough away that no invented interior or person appears. Natural fading light, historically modest construction, no minarets, domes, modern Ka'bah reconstruction, or people.

Review each frame for historical plausibility, unintended figures or faces, anachronisms, excessive spectacle, readable text, and a calm tone. Keep the current SVGs until the human visual review is complete.
